High precision images¶

Eight bits per channel show visible steps in slow, dark gradients, and they cannot carry HDR video as it is produced. Silkie accepts three raw pixel formats with more bits per channel through the existing kitty graphics protocol, and a key that says which transfer function the values are encoded with, so that an HDR10 frame (ten-bit PQ in Rec. 2020) can be sent as it comes out of a decoder:

The numbers follow the scheme of Packed image formats: the base of the family (300 gray, 600 RGBA) plus the bits per pixel. Silkie keeps these pixels at their full precision all the way to the GPU.

Discovery¶

The 扩展能力协议 lists 316, 632 and 664 in its format field. A feature query reports a format and the transfer key together, for example:

<ESC>_Ga=q,i=31,e=t,f=632,E=16,G=2<ESC>\

is answered with <ESC>_Gi=31,e=t,f=632,G=2,E=16;OK<ESC>\ by a terminal that shows HDR10 images. A terminal that does not know the E key may reject a command carrying it without an answer, so ask first and send the key only when it is echoed back.

Byte order and layout¶

Every multi-byte value is little-endian, as the half floats of f=64 and the words of RGB565 are. That is also the order in which GPUs and every CPU Silkie runs on hold these formats, so the bytes go to the GPU without being converted. Pixels run left to right, rows top to bottom, with no padding between rows: the payload is s * v * bytes_per_pixel bytes, before any o=z compression. Width and height are required in s and v.

f=316, 16-bit gray

One unsigned 16-bit value per pixel, 0 black and 65535 white. The value 0x8000 is sent as the bytes 00 80. Gray has no gamut, the G key is ignored.

f=632, RGB10A2

One 32-bit word per pixel, with red in bits 0–9, green in bits 10–19, blue in bits 20–29 and alpha in bits 30–31:

word = r | (g << 10) | (b << 20) | (a << 30)

This is the layout of OpenGL's GL_RGB10_A2 with GL_UNSIGNED_INT_2_10_10_10_REV, Vulkan's VK_FORMAT_A2B10G10R10_UNORM_PACK32, Metal's rgb10a2Unorm and DXGI's R10G10B10A2_UNORM, so a frame from any of them can be sent unchanged. Worked examples, as they appear on the wire:

Alpha is straight, not pre-multiplied, as for f=32.

f=664, 16-bit RGBA

Four unsigned 16-bit values per pixel, red, green, blue and alpha in that order, each little-endian. Opaque white is eight ff bytes; red at full intensity and half opacity is ff ff 00 00 00 00 00 80. Alpha is straight.

The transfer function: the E key¶

The values of a raw image are encoded with the sRGB curve, as they always were. The E key names another curve, with the code points of ITU-T H.273, which HDR video, AVIF and the cICP chunk of PNG use as well:

E

Transfer function

0

No key: sRGB, the default.

13

sRGB, the same as no key.

8

Linear: the values are proportional to light, 1.0 (the largest value) is SDR white.

16

PQ, SMPTE ST 2084: absolute luminance up to 10000 nits.

18

HLG, ITU-R BT.2100 hybrid log-gamma.

Any other value is refused with EINVAL. The key belongs in the first chunk of a chunked transmission. It applies to the formats above and to every other raw format of RGB, RGBA or gray values: f=24, f=32 and the packed formats of Packed image formats, which are expanded to eight bits first and keep the curve. f=64 is linear by definition: E=8 is accepted for it and changes nothing, any other value is refused. The key is refused for f=100 (a PNG says what it is itself), for f=8 and f=16 (their colors are those of the terminal), for the palette images f=401 to f=408 of Palette images (their palettes are sRGB), for f=200 (each paint of a vector image names its own colors) and for GPU surfaces, t=g.

Silkie's linear values have SDR white, the white of ordinary text, at 1.0, and show what is brighter with the headroom of the display, see HDR 亮度协议. PQ is absolute, so Silkie takes the reference white of ITU-R BT.2408, 203 nits, to be SDR white: a PQ value of 203 nits is shown as bright as text, 406 nits at twice that, and 1000 nits at 4.93 times, which the display shows if it has that much headroom and clips otherwise. HLG is relative; Silkie shows it as BT.2100 does on a display with a nominal peak of 1000 nits (system gamma 1.2), which puts the reference white of HLG, a signal of 0.75, at 203 nits and so at SDR white, and the largest signal at 4.93 times SDR white. The luminance HLG's system gamma is applied to uses the primaries of the image, Rec. 2020 when G=2.

The G key of 广色域颜色 combines with E: an image is decoded with its curve, then converted from its gamut to that of the window. HDR10 is f=632,E=16,G=2. Colors of a PQ or HLG image keep their brightness above SDR white through the conversion; colors outside the gamut of the window are clipped to it. T=1 (tone mapping, see HDR 亮度协议) works with PQ and HLG images: the peak it compresses from is the brightest decoded value of the image.

Choosing a format¶

f=632

Ten bits per channel in four bytes, the size of f=32. Enough for smooth SDR gradients, and with E=16 or E=18 the format of HDR video. Its two bits of alpha suit opaque frames and hard masks, not soft edges.

f=664

Sixteen bits per channel and full alpha, in eight bytes. For gradients and masks rendered at high precision, for linear light (E=8) where eight or ten bits leave the shadows coarse, and for PQ or HLG content with soft alpha.

f=316

Sixteen-bit gray in two bytes, for scientific and medical images, depth maps and monochrome HDR.

f=64

Half floats in eight bytes, linear extended sRGB: for values that are already linear floats, values above SDR white without a curve, and colors outside every gamut (negative components). It is also the only high precision format that animation frames can use.

Animation frames and composition (a=f and a=c) are not supported for the formats above, nor for an image with a transfer function other than sRGB; the terminal answers EINVAL. Use f=64 for animations that need more than eight bits.

In Silkie¶

The pixels are kept as they were sent, and uploaded to textures of their own precision: GL_R16 for 16-bit gray, GL_RGB10_A2 for RGB10A2 and GL_RGBA16 for 16-bit RGBA. All three are required, filterable formats of the desktop OpenGL 3.1 and later that Silkie draws with, on macOS, Linux and the BSDs. An eight bit image with a transfer function other than sRGB goes into an GL_RGBA8 texture rather than an sRGB one. The graphics shader expands gray to RGB and decodes the curve into Silkie's linear, extended-range values, so a slow gradient reaches an HDR window, which has a floating point framebuffer, without being cut to eight bits. A window without HDR stores eight bits per channel, and other output paths are unchanged. When an image is scaled, the shader decodes each of the neighbouring pixels and interpolates in linear light, as the GPU does for sRGB textures, so that edges and gradients are not darkened by interpolating encoded values. When an image is shrunk, the pixels a screen pixel covers are averaged, up to four in each direction. An image drawn at its natural size needs no interpolation.

Vector images that draw one of these images (img= in 矢量图像) receive its decoded linear values as half floats. Pointer images (the pointer image protocol) are SDR: values brighter than SDR white are shown at SDR white.
